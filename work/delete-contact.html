Delete Contact

Deleting a contact on iPhone hides behind Edit and a long scroll. I proposed two faster routes that reuse patterns people already know.

Why delete a contact?

We all have one we should let go

  • Decided it’s time to move on?

    Deleting your ex’s number is a good first step.

  • Who’s this again?

    If you can’t remember, maybe it’s time to delete.

  • That delivery guy from 2 years ago?

    Yeah, you can let that one go.

The problem

Should you Edit a contact to Delete it?

I recently wanted to delete an unwanted contact, something that should’ve been simple. But to my surprise, I struggled. It felt oddly frustrating not being able to complete such a basic task without second-guessing.

Curious if it was just me, I asked 12 friends and colleagues who use iPhones. Turns out, I wasn’t alone. 11 of 12 either got it wrong or admitted they had to figure it out on the spot. The process didn’t feel natural or intuitive to most.

The solutions

Two routes, two real needs

Story 1

After a tough break-up, I want to delete my ex’s contact, since I’m fairly certain of their details, as a step to move on.

  1. Contacts
  2. Swipe
  3. Delete contact

3sdown from 12s

Swipe to delete straight from the list, the same pattern people use in Messages. A confirmation prevents accidental deletion.

Story 2

While browsing my contacts, I want to delete an unwanted one after checking its details, to keep my list up to date.

  1. Contacts
  2. Contact details
  3. Scroll
  4. Delete contact

6sdown from 12s

Edit and Delete become separate actions on the contact page, so you can delete the right contact without editing it first. A confirmation still guards against mistakes.

The impact

Faster, and it finally makes sense

  • Up to 75%

    Less time to reach the goal

    And the saving compounds when deleting several contacts.

  • Matches what people expect

    The result lines up with what people try first, which removes friction.

  • Familiar iOS patterns

    Reusing patterns from across iOS shortens the learning curve (Jakob’s Law).

  • Minimal development effort

    Existing components can be reused to build it.
